Parking

Today's free ride: because it is Labor Day, Los Angeles does not enforce parking meters, posted time limits, or street sweeping. Street parking in Hollywood is free all day. One catch — if a sign reads “Holidays Enforced”, it means it. Glance at the meter screen before walking off.

Hollywood Boulevard

Side streets: Orange Dr, Hawthorn Ave, Sycamore Ave, Yucca St. If they're full, Ovation Hollywood garage is $25 daily max — or $3 for four hours if you validate at the TCL Chinese Theatre box office.

Hollywood Forever

Free lot inside the gates. Enter off Santa Monica Blvd and drive in past the flower shop.

Lake Hollywood Park

Free on Canyon Lake Dr, 6 am–8 pm on the unlimited side. The opposite side is 15-minute only and they do tow.

Griffith Observatory — do not drive to the top

The Observatory Road lot is $10 per hour, card only, enforced Monday 12 pm–10 pm, and the rate goes up on holidays. Four hours is roughly $40, and on Labor Day at sunset you will still circle for half an hour.

Instead: park free in the Greek Theatre lot, 2700 N Vermont Canyon Rd — there is no performance there tonight — and take the DASH Observatory shuttle to the front lawn. 50¢ cash, 35¢ with a TAP card, every 15–20 minutes, 10 am–10 pm. It runs on Mondays even though the building is shut. Last bus down from the Observatory leaves at 10 pm.

Shooting notes — X100VI

Film simulations

Classic Chrome for the Boulevard and all the architecture — it holds the stone and the marquee paint without going candy. Acros + red filter inside the mausoleum. Switch to Nostalgic Neg at 6 pm; it carries golden hour without tipping orange.

The 23mm problem

Your lens is a 35mm equivalent — wide for portraits, and it will stretch her features if you fill the frame. Use the digital teleconverter at 50mm for every close-up of her, and stay native 23mm for facades, the forecourt and the domes.

Exposure by block

Morning, open shade: f/5.6, ISO 160, +0.3 EV — she's shaded against bright stone, so the meter will underexpose her face.

Golden hour, Griffith: f/2, expose for her face and let the sky clip a little. Backlight from the west with the domes behind her.

Blue hour, no tripod: f/2, ISO 1600, 1/60. Brace on the terrace railing and let IBIS do the rest. Shoot bursts and keep the sharpest.

Bag

Water for both of you, blotting sheets, a hand fan, a phone battery pack, a spare camera battery, and one light layer for the hilltop. It is one outfit and a fourteen-hour day at 87°F — the heat is the real adversary, not the light.
